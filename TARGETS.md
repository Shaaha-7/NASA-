## 0. Important context change (affects our story)

In **February 2026 NASA restructured Artemis**:
- **Artemis III** (summer 2027) no longer lands. It is now a crewed test flight in Earth orbit (docking with the landers, suits, life support). [S1][S2]
- The **first crewed Moon landing is now Artemis IV, planned for early 2028, near the lunar south pole.** [S1][S2]
- NASA's **9 candidate landing regions** (announced Oct 2024 for Artemis III) are still the working list. They are reported to carry over to Artemis IV, but **NASA has not published a new list**, so we say "Artemis candidate landing regions," not "Artemis III." [S3][S4]

**For our pitch:** the first human landing in 50+ years is ~2 years away, and crews must train on Earth before then. That makes our project timely.

## 1. MOON: Mons Mouton (lunar south pole)

### Location (official)
| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Official name | Mons Mouton (IAU Feature ID 16070) | [S5] |
| Center | **84.60° S, 31.00° E** | [S5] |
| Extent | 83.30° S to 86.22° S; 11.67° E to 53.03° E | [S5] |
| Diameter | **130 km** (IAU outline) | [S5] |
| Coordinate system | Planetographic, +East, LOLA 2011 control network | [S5] |
| Candidate regions | Two of NASA's 9: **"Mons Mouton"** and **"Mons Mouton Plateau"** | [S3] |

### Why this target
- A **flat-topped mountain** between Nobile and Malapert craters, within 6° of the south pole. [S6]
- The **Mons Mouton Plateau has the largest suitable landing area of all 9 candidate regions: about 565 km²** after slope and landing-accuracy filtering. [S7]
- Its high summit gets **near-continuous sunlight** (good for solar power). [S8]
- **Small permanently shadowed regions (PSRs)** nearby, about 1 km² each, may trap water ice. [S9]
- Was the planned landing area of NASA's VIPER ice rover (cancelled July 2024). [S10]

### Fingerprint (what the place is like)
| Feature | Value | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gravity | 1.62 m/s² (0.17 g) | Verified | [S11] |
| Atmosphere | None (vacuum) | Verified | [S11] |
| Sun angle | Sun always near the horizon (Moon's axis is tilted only ~1.5°) → very long shadows | Verified (general) | [S11] |
| Height | Top surface about 6 km above lunar datum (estimates vary by definition) | Approximate | [S6] |
| Cold traps | PSR floors as cold as ~35–40 K (−238 °C to −233 °C) at the south pole generally | Verified (south pole, not Mons Mouton specifically) | [S12] |
| Surface temperature on the plateau | **TO MEASURE** from Diviner data in Phase 2 | Pending | [D1] |
| Slope distribution | **TO MEASURE** from LOLA elevation in Phase 2 | Pending | [D2] |
| Rock type | Highlands terrain (light, feldspar-rich rock, anorthosite). Detailed geology mapped in a 2025 paper | General; details to read | [S13] |
| Ice | Possible in PSRs, not confirmed at the surface | Possible | [S9] |

### What Earth CAN match (we score these)
Flat-topped high ground · slopes · very low sun and long shadows (high-latitude Earth) · extreme cold (polar/high-altitude Earth) · frozen ground · feldspar-rich rock (anorthosite outcrops on Earth).

### What Earth CANNOT match (we always state these)
Gravity (0.17 g) · vacuum · cold traps at ~40 K · sharp, unweathered Moon dust · radiation · 2-week day/night cycle at low latitudes (different at the poles).

## 2. MARS: Arcadia Planitia (subsurface ice plain)

### Location (official)
| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Official name | Arcadia Planitia (IAU Feature ID 348) | [S14] |
| Center | **46.66° N, 192.00° E** (= 168.00° W) | [S14] |
| Extent | 34.68° N to 54.68° N; 165° E to 217° E | [S14] |
| Size | ~2,200 km across | [S14] |
| Coordinate system | Planetocentric, +East, 0–360° | [S14] |

**Our specific target spots:** the three top-ranked candidate human landing sites from a 2025 peer-reviewed study, at the Arcadia / northern Amazonis boundary: [S15]

| Site | Latitude | Longitude | Max relief inside HiRISE image |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AP-1** | 39.80° N | 202.10° E | 31 m |
| **AP-8** | 40.75° N | 201.30° E | 140 m (only 8 m in the central 2.5 km²) |
| **AP-9** | 40.02° N | 203.35° E | 10 m |

### Why this target
- **Ice only ~8–12 cm below the surface**, estimated from the size of ~9,000 mapped ground-crack polygons (calibrated with Antarctica and NASA's Phoenix site). Shallow enough to dig up for water. [S15]
- Ice-rich ground in parts of Arcadia extends **up to ~100 m deep**. [S16]
- **Very flat**: AP-9 has only 10 m of relief across its HiRISE image. [S15]
- The most-cited region for future human landings. [S15][S17]

|---|---|---|---|
| Gravity | 3.71 m/s² (0.38 g) | Verified | [S18] |
| Air pressure (Mars average) | ~6.4 mbar, under 1% of Earth's | Verified (global mean) | [S18] |
| Air pressure at Arcadia | **TO MEASURE** from Mars Climate Database (lowland, so likely above average) | Pending | [D3] |
| Temperature range | **TO MEASURE** from Mars Climate Database | Pending | [D3] |
| Ice depth | ~8–12 cm at AP sites | Verified | [S15] |
| Ground pattern | Thermal-contraction polygons (the same kind as in Earth's frozen ground) | Verified | [S15] |
| Terrain | Flat plains, very low relief | Verified | [S14][S15] |
| Dust | **TO MEASURE** from Mars Climate Database | Pending | [D3] |
| Ice map | **TO DOWNLOAD** from SWIM | Pending | [D4] |

### What Earth CAN match
Shallow ground ice (permafrost) · polygon-cracked frozen ground · flat, cold, dry plains · drilling and digging conditions.

### What Earth CANNOT match
Gravity (0.38 g) · air pressure (<1% of Earth's) · CO₂ air · ice that turns straight to gas when exposed · radiation · UV.

## 3. MARS: Jezero Crater (ancient lake, rover site)

### Location (official)
| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Official name | Jezero (IAU Feature ID 14300) | [S19] |
| Center | **18.41° N, 77.69° E** | [S19] |
| Extent | 18.01° N to 18.81° N; 77.27° E to 78.11° E | [S19] |
| Diameter | **47.52 km** | [S19] |
| Coordinate system | Planetocentric, +East, 0–360° | [S19] |

### Why this target
- An **ancient lake** with a river **delta**, inside the western edge of the giant Isidis impact basin. [S20]
- Rocks with **olivine and carbonate minerals**, seen from orbit (CRISM) and studied by the rover. Carbonates can preserve signs of past life. [S20]
- NASA's **Perseverance rover** has been driving there since February 2021, so we have **real ground measurements** to check our method against. [S21]

### Fingerprint
| Feature | Value | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gravity | 3.71 m/s² (0.38 g) | Verified | [S18] |
| Average air temperature | about **−55 °C**, with **50–60 °C** day–night swings | Verified (MEDA instrument, press summary of paper) | [S22] |
| Example day | High −22 °C, low −83 °C, pressure 718 Pa (sols 43–44) | Verified (single days, press report) | [S23] |
| Minerals | Olivine, carbonates (delta and crater floor) | Verified | [S20] |
| Terrain | Crater floor, delta front, crater rim | Verified | [S20] |
| Dust devils | Frequent; pressure drops of ~0.3–6.5 Pa | Verified | [S24] |
| Slopes | **TO MEASURE** from Mars elevation data | Pending | [D5] |

### What Earth CAN match
Ancient lake and delta landforms · olivine and carbonate rocks · dry, cold, dusty desert · crater geometry.

### What Earth CANNOT match
Gravity (0.38 g) · air pressure (~0.7% of Earth's) · CO₂ air · radiation · UV.

### Known Earth analog (for our proof test)
**Lake Salda, Turkey** has carbonate rocks and a delta-like shoreline that NASA scientists have compared to Jezero. **UNVERIFIED in this file:** confirm source before using in the pitch.

## 4. Comparison rules (how Earth gets compared to these targets)

1. **Never compare raw values that cannot overlap.** Earth has no 40 K ground. We compare **patterns and rankings** instead: "among the coldest 1% of land on its planet," "slope in the same range."
2. **Same scale.** Slopes from Earth and Moon/Mars elevation maps are compared at the same grid size.
3. **Mission decides weight.** Rover: slope and ground first. Ice drill: frozen ground first. Astronaut: terrain, cold, low sun, remoteness.
4. **Always list what does not match** (sections above).
